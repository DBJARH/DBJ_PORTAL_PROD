Taxonomy-Core

DBJ Enterprise Taxonomy — the Core
Dusan B. Jovanovic

Taxonomy might be explained as the terminology of the Information Space Positioning System (ISPS). Example Taxonomy Plaque:

Category: Implementation
Capability: Development

The vocabulary on that plaque is small, rigid and well defined.

With this plaque on the content, both consumers and producers of the content know what the tagged content is all about. And the author does not need to explain it to every reader.

Important This document is single source of truth.

It contains the taxonomy hierarchy and the terminology defined by it.

These definitions are to be used company wide, with no changes or reinterpretations. Ideally industry wide

  • Common, shared and simple Taxonomy is the underlying essential mesh holding the IT organization landscape together.
  • It gives both the structure and naming of structure elements.
  • It is the essential common language of the organization {: .important}

Important Taxonomy does not define what is it that the organization actually does or produces. {: .important}

Taxonomy Schema#

DBJ Taxonomy is hierarchy. This schema definition is visual.

├── Category
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   └── Capability
├── Category
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   └── Capability
├── Category
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   ├── Capability
│   └── Capability
└── Category
    ├── Capability
    ├── Capability
    ├── Capability
    └── Capability

First level are categories, second level are capabilities.

That is the “schema” not the Taxonomy itself. Taxonomy Schema is made of only two elements

  1. Category
    1. Tope level classification. Only four Categories total.
    2. We divide the whole operational space of the IT based company in these four categories.
    3. In schema we do not say what are they, we just define there is four of them
  2. Capability
    1. Each Category has total four offspring nodes: Capabilities. That makes 16 Capabilities, all together
    2. In schema we do not say what are they, we just define there is four of them per each category.

Thus we say: Taxonomy entities are uniquely defined by two schema elements: Category and Capability.

Schema is data description. Data about the data aka “meta data”. Schema will be required by tools operating and the DBJ Taxonomy.

Warning Names of the nodes on the Taxonomy hierarchy are official Organization Terminology (aka Vocabulary). {: .warning}

Important Entities in the core are non-divisible information space, core particles. Thus they are axioms of communication for the standard modern organization focused on producing software products. {: .important}

Vocabulary/Terminology are official words. Please do not use synonyms. That will dilute their unique meaning defined by the taxonomy.

Vocabulary#

Information Space Positioning System (ISPS)#

The organization’s information space is a space in the literal sense: content sits somewhere in it, and without coordinates nobody can say where. The Taxonomy supplies those coordinates. Category and Capability are the two axes; together they fix a position.

A Taxonomy Plaque is a position expressed on a piece of content — the equivalent of a coordinate reading. It states where the content sits, not what it says.

The analogy to satellite positioning is deliberate and bounded: a small fixed vocabulary, applied uniformly, yields an unambiguous position for anything within the space. It does not imply navigation, distance, or movement between positions.

Schema#

The shape of the hierarchy, independent of the names on its nodes. It fixes the depth (two levels), the arity (four by four), and the two element kinds — Category and Capability — but says nothing about what any node is called.

The schema is the invariant. The DBJ Core Taxonomy is one naming of it, for the context of a commercial enterprise based on IT.

The “abstract to concrete” progression#

Traces to Aristotle’s Metaphysics and Posterior Analytics — the idea that genuine knowledge (episteme) moves from universals (genus, form) down to particulars. Aristotle’s hierarchy of genus → species → individual is the direct ancestor of every taxonomic tree since.

Including Porphyry’s Tree (3rd century AD, Porphyry’s commentary on Aristotle’s Categories) — a top-down branching hierarchy from substance down to individual, structurally identical.
